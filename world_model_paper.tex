\begin{abstract}
\noindent
World models—learned simulators of environment dynamics used in model-based
reinforcement learning—have demonstrated strong empirical performance yet
remain difficult to endow with machine-checked invariants. We argue that
dependent type theory, and in particular the algebraic structures made
tractable in the Rocq proof assistant, provides a natural semantic framework
for strengthening world models with verifiable guarantees. We introduce the
notion of a \emph{proof-carrying world model}: a learned simulator whose
latent states, observation operators, and planning procedures are annotated
with machine-checked witnesses of admissibility. We show how partial
commutative monoids (PCMs) over finitely keyed maps—already developed for
separation-logic reasoning about heap-like resources—can be reinterpreted as
a compositional semantics for heterogeneous agent histories, cached beliefs,
and capability scopes. We develop small illustrative Rocq formalizations of
these ideas and sketch a research agenda toward alignment-aware planning
under typed resource constraints.
\end{abstract}

\bigskip
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} world models, dependent type theory, Rocq, partial
commutative monoids, model-based reinforcement learning, proof-carrying
programs, alignment.

\section{Introduction}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The success of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) over the past decade
rests on a single insight: an agent that can simulate its environment can plan
efficiently without exhausting the real environment in trial
\citep{sutton1990dyna, ha2018world, hafner2019dream, schrittwieser2020mastering}.
The learned simulator—often called a \emph{world model}—compresses sensory
history into a compact latent state, predicts the consequences of candidate
actions, and thereby enables forward search at a fraction of the sample cost
required by purely reactive approaches
\citep{hafner2023mastering, ye2021efficientzero}.

Despite this empirical progress, world models share a structural limitation
with most learned systems: their internal representations carry no
machine-verifiable account of what they are permitted to represent or
what invariants they are committed to preserving. Admissibility conditions,
scope constraints, and capability limits are expressed, when they are expressed
at all, in natural language documentation, external monitors, or post-hoc
audits. They are, in the language of software engineering, \emph{extrinsic} to
the model rather than \emph{intrinsic} to it.

This paper argues that dependent type theory provides the missing vocabulary.
In a dependently typed setting, an object may carry, as part of its very type,
a machine-checked proof that it satisfies the conditions required for its use.
Proof need not arrive as a chaperone after the fact; it can accompany the value
from the moment of construction. We draw in particular on the Rocq proof
assistant \citep{coq2024} and on the FCSL-PCM library \citep{nanevski2019fcsl},
which develops partial commutative monoids (PCMs) as a clean algebraic model
of separable, composable resources.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A conceptual framework—the \emph{proof-carrying world model}—that
    articulates how dependent types can annotate the operational interfaces of
    a learned simulator (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
  \item An account of how PCMs, originally developed for separation-logic
    reasoning over heap memory, can be reinterpreted as a compositional
    semantics for agent observation histories and capability scopes
    (\S\ref{sec:pcm}).
  \item Small Rocq formalizations illustrating these ideas, from basic
    proof-carrying state through typed rollout traces to heap-style ownership
    of sensor boundaries (\S\ref{sec:rocq}).
  \item A discussion of research directions, including connections to
    alignment-aware planning and multi-agent trust (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

We do not claim a complete implementation. We offer a research direction and
a working vocabulary for future development.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{World Models in Reinforcement Learning}

A world model, in the MBRL sense, is a parametric function
$f_\theta : \mathcal{Z} \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{Z} \times \mathcal{R}$
mapping a latent state $z \in \mathcal{Z}$ and action $a \in \mathcal{A}$ to a
predicted successor state and reward signal. Practical systems augment this
with encoder and decoder networks that map between observation space and latent
space, and with planning modules—cross-entropy method \citep{botev2013cem},
Monte Carlo Tree Search \citep{coulom2006mcts}, or learned value functions
\citep{hafner2023mastering}—that search over imagined trajectories.

Key empirical milestones include Dyna \citep{sutton1990dyna}, which first
demonstrated the efficiency of planning inside a learned model; World Models
\citep{ha2018world}, which popularized recurrent variational world models for
visual tasks; DreamerV1–V3 \citep{hafner2019dream, hafner2021mastering,
hafner2023mastering}, which showed that long-horizon imagination can match or
exceed model-free baselines; and MuZero \citep{schrittwieser2020mastering},
which learned a model sufficient for planning without requiring faithful
reconstruction of observations.

A recurring theme is the tension between \emph{predictive accuracy} and
\emph{structural fidelity}. Predictively accurate models may still fail to
capture the constraints—resource limits, scope boundaries, capability
revocations—that govern what a system is permitted to do, as distinct from
what it expects to happen. This gap is the starting point for our proposal.

\subsection{Dependent Type Theory and the Rocq Proof Assistant}

Dependent type theory \citep{martin-lof1984, coquand1988calculus} extends the
Curry–Howard correspondence \citep{howard1980, wadler2015propositions} so that
types may contain, and depend upon, ordinary program values. A type such as
$\mathsf{Vec}(n)$—a vector of exactly $n$ elements—encodes a length invariant
that the type-checker enforces at compile time, without separate runtime
assertion. More generally, a value of type $\{x : A \mid P(x)\}$ is both a
value in $A$ and a proof of $P(x)$, inseparably.

Rocq (formerly Coq) \citep{coq2024} implements the Calculus of Inductive
Constructions \citep{coquand1988calculus, paulin-mohring2015}, a rich
dependent type theory supporting:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Inductive types} and pattern matching;
  \item \emph{Dependent records} (\texttt{Record}) that bundle values with
    their correctness conditions;
  \item \emph{Proof terms} as first-class citizens of the same language as
    programs;
  \item \emph{Extraction} to OCaml or Haskell, enabling certified programs to
    run outside the proof environment.
\end{itemize}

The MathComp library \citep{gonthier2013odd} and the FCSL-PCM library
\citep{nanevski2019fcsl} provide mature algebraic foundations—including Boolean
reflection, finite maps, and partial commutative monoids—on which we build.

\subsection{Partial Commutative Monoids}

\begin{definition}[Partial Commutative Monoid]
A \emph{partial commutative monoid} (PCM) is a set $U$ equipped with a
distinguished element $\mathbf{0} \in U$, a \emph{validity} predicate
$\mathsf{valid} : U \to \mathsf{Prop}$, and a partial binary operation
$\oplus : U \to U \to U$ satisfying:
\begin{align*}
  &\mathsf{commutativity:}    & x \oplus y &= y \oplus x \\
  &\mathsf{associativity:}    & (x \oplus y) \oplus z &= x \oplus (y \oplus z) \\
  &\mathsf{unit:}             & x \oplus \mathbf{0} &= x \\
  &\mathsf{validity\ domain:} & \mathsf{valid}(x \oplus y) &\Rightarrow
    \mathsf{valid}(x) \wedge \mathsf{valid}(y)
\end{align*}
When $\mathsf{valid}(x \oplus y)$ fails, $x \oplus y$ returns a distinguished
\emph{undefined} element $\mathsf{Undef}$.
\end{definition}

PCMs arose in the context of separation logic \citep{reynolds2002sl,
ohearn2019sl} as the canonical semantic domain for \emph{separating
conjunction}: two resources $x$ and $y$ may be combined if and only if their
join is valid—that is, they do not overlap or conflict. The FCSL-PCM library
\citep{nanevski2019fcsl} provides a rich Rocq hierarchy of PCM instances,
including heap-like finite maps, which we repurpose in \S\ref{sec:pcm}.

\section{A Framework for Proof-Carrying World Models}
\label{sec:framework}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Motivation}

The latent state $z$ of a world model typically has no intrinsic type other
than ``tensor of floats.'' Its admissibility—whether it encodes a state the
system is permitted to reason about—is enforced, if at all, by external
wrappers, evaluation callbacks, or human review. We propose to make
admissibility \emph{intrinsic}: the type of a latent state should include
evidence of its own legitimacy.

More concretely, consider a multi-agent planning system in which different
submodels govern different capability scopes—one model has read access to
sensor $S_1$, another controls actuator $A_3$. In a conventional architecture,
scope enforcement is handled by runtime checks, policy tables, or
application-level guards that sit outside the model itself. A dependently typed
architecture would express scope as part of the type of the state: the type of
``state with actuator $A_3$ capability'' literally differs from the type of
``state without it.'' Scope confusion becomes a type error, detected before
execution.

\subsection{Proof-Carrying State}

We define a \emph{proof-carrying state} as a dependent record bundling a
representational value with a machine-checked admissibility witness.

\begin{definition}[Proof-Carrying State]
Let $S$ be a set of representational states and let
$\mathsf{Adm} : S \to \mathsf{Prop}$ be an admissibility predicate. A
\emph{proof-carrying state} is a pair $(s, h)$ where $s \in S$ and $h :
\mathsf{Adm}(s)$.
\end{definition}

The key property is that proof-carrying states \emph{cannot be constructed in
an inadmissible configuration}: construction of the pair requires producing the
proof $h$ simultaneously with the value $s$. There is no window between
construction and certification.

\subsection{Proof-Carrying World Model Interface}

A world model operating over proof-carrying states must be designed so that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Observation closure}: applying the observation operator to an
    admissible state produces an admissible state.
  \item \emph{Imagination validity}: applying the imagination (rollout)
    operator to an admissible state produces a valid (non-undefined) state.
\end{enumerate}

These are not runtime checks but proof obligations discharged at definition
time. A world model that cannot satisfy them is, in a precise sense,
\emph{ill-typed}—it cannot be instantiated.

The following Rocq sketch captures the interface:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Proof-carrying world model interface in Rocq},
                   label={lst:world-interface}]
From mathcomp Require Import ssreflect ssrbool ssrnat seq.
From pcm Require Import pcm.

Section TypedWorld.
Context {State : pcm}.

Record WorldModel := {
  observe  : State -> seq nat -> State;
  imagine  : State -> seq nat -> State;
  admissible : State -> Prop;
  observe_ok : forall s frame,
    admissible s -> admissible (observe s frame);
  imagine_ok : forall s actions,
    admissible s -> valid (imagine s actions)
}.

End TypedWorld.
\end{lstlisting}

The \texttt{State} is parameterized by an arbitrary PCM, so the framework is
not committed to any particular representation; heap-like finite maps, trace
histories, and richer algebraic structures are all valid instantiations.

\section{PCMs as Compositional Semantics for Agent Histories}
\label{sec:pcm}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{From Heap Reasoning to History Composition}

Separation logic was designed to reason locally: a Hoare triple in separation
logic is valid for a fragment of the heap, and the frame rule extends it to
arbitrarily larger heaps that the procedure does not touch
\citep{reynolds2002sl}. The key semantic enabler is that heap composition via
$\oplus$ is defined only when the constituent fragments are disjoint—capturing
the intuition that two independent computations cannot silently share state.

We propose an analogous discipline for world model histories. An
\emph{observation history} is a finite map from timesteps to observation
records; a \emph{capability scope} is a finite map from capability identifiers
to authorization witnesses. Two such fragments can be joined if and only if
their key sets are disjoint—that is, if they record disjoint portions of the
agent's experience or authority.

\begin{definition}[History PCM]
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a countable set of timesteps and $\mathcal{O}$ a set of
observation records. Define $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{T} \rightharpoonup
\mathcal{O}$ (partial functions with finite support). The PCM structure on
$\mathcal{H}$ is:
\begin{align*}
  \mathsf{valid}(h) &\;\triangleq\; h \neq \mathsf{Undef} \\
  h_1 \oplus h_2 &\;\triangleq\;
    \begin{cases}
      h_1 \cup h_2 & \text{if } \mathrm{dom}(h_1) \cap \mathrm{dom}(h_2) = \emptyset \\
      \mathsf{Undef} & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
This is exactly the PCM of disjoint-key finite maps, realized in Rocq by
\texttt{natmap} \citep{nanevski2019fcsl}.
\end{definition}

Under this reading, combining two histories is only valid if they record
distinct moments in time. Conflicting entries—two agents writing different
observations for the same timestep—produce an undefined result, signaling a
representational conflict that the planner must not silently resolve.

\subsection{Typed Rollout Traces}

A \emph{rollout trace} in our framework is a proof-carrying history: a finite
map from timesteps to rewards, annotated with a positive-horizon witness. The
following Rocq definition makes this concrete:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Typed rollout trace},
                   label={lst:rollout}]
From mathcomp Require Import ssreflect ssrbool ssrnat seq.
From pcm Require Import pcm natmap.

Record rollout := {
  trace      : history nat;
  horizon    : nat;
  horizon_pos : horizon > 0
}.

Definition extend_trace (r : rollout) (t reward : nat) :=
  if t == 0 then None
  else if valid ((t \-> reward) \+ trace r)
       then Some {| trace      := (t \-> reward) \+ trace r;
                    horizon    := horizon r;
                    horizon_pos := horizon_pos r |}
       else None.
\end{lstlisting}

The \texttt{extend\_trace} function is partial: it returns \texttt{None}
whenever the new timestep is zero (a sentinel for invalid time) or whenever
the new entry conflicts with an existing entry in the map. This partiality is
not an error condition to be caught at runtime; it is the honest signature of
an operation that may legitimately fail to produce a valid result.

\subsection{Capability Scopes as Resource Types}

Beyond observation histories, PCMs provide a natural model for
\emph{capability scopes}—the set of actions an agent is currently authorized
to perform. We represent a capability scope as a finite map from capability
identifiers to authorization tokens (represented, for simplicity, as natural
numbers). Two scopes may be combined only if they govern disjoint sets of
capabilities. An agent attempting to act under a capability it does not hold
faces a type error at the point of action construction, not a runtime
permission denial.

This design separates two concerns that are often conflated in practice:
\emph{what an agent expects to happen} (the predictive content of the world
model) and \emph{what an agent is permitted to cause} (the normative content,
now encoded in types). Violations of the latter are detected constructively,
before any real-world action is taken.

\section{Illustrative Rocq Formalizations}
\label{sec:rocq}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Proof-Carrying State: A Minimal Example}

Before introducing PCM structure, we illustrate the simplest instance of
proof-carrying state: a rollout record whose \texttt{horizon} field is
certified by a length witness.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Minimal proof-carrying rollout record},
                   label={lst:minimal}]
From Coq Require Import List Arith.
Import ListNotations.

Record CheckedRollout := {
  horizon         : nat;
  actions         : list nat;
  horizon_matches : length actions = horizon
}.

Definition single_action (a : nat) : CheckedRollout :=
  {| horizon         := 1;
     actions         := [a];
     horizon_matches := eq_refl |}.
\end{lstlisting}

The field \texttt{horizon\_matches} is not a runtime assertion but a proof
term. It is discharged by \texttt{eq\_refl} at the point of construction,
confirming that the singleton list \texttt{[a]} has length~1. A \texttt{CheckedRollout}
with mismatched horizon cannot be constructed; the attempt would produce a type
error.

\subsection{Sensor Ownership via Heap-Style PCMs}

We illustrate resource-typed sensor access using the \texttt{heap} PCM from
FCSL-PCM. A sensor write is defined to return \texttt{Undef} if the target
pointer is null, and a lemma certifies that non-null writes preserve validity.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Heap-style ownership for sensor boundary},
                   label={lst:sensor}]
From mathcomp Require Import ssreflect ssrbool.
From pcm Require Import heap.

Definition sensor_write (p : ptr) (v : dynamic id) (h : heap) :=
  if p == null then Undef else upd p v h.

Lemma sensor_write_preserves_nonnull p v h :
  p != null ->
  sensor_write p v h != Undef.
Proof.
move=> p_nonnull.
rewrite /sensor_write p_nonnull.
case: h=> //= fm pf.
by case: decP=> // /eqP.
Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

The lemma \texttt{sensor\_write\_preserves\_nonnull} is a machine-checked
guarantee. Any system that calls \texttt{sensor\_write} on a non-null pointer
inherits the proof; any downstream code that requires a valid heap fragment
can accept the result directly.

\subsection{Composing Agent Histories}

The following sketch demonstrates how two disjoint history fragments may be
safely combined into a single rollout.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Safe composition of disjoint observation fragments},
                   label={lst:compose}]
From mathcomp Require Import ssreflect ssrbool ssrnat.
From pcm Require Import pcm natmap.

(* Two disjoint observation fragments *)
Definition obs1 : history nat := 1 \-> 42.
Definition obs2 : history nat := 2 \-> 17.

(* Their join is valid because key sets are disjoint *)
Lemma obs_join_valid : valid (obs1 \+ obs2).
Proof. by rewrite /obs1 /obs2; compute. Qed.

(* An attempt to join conflicting entries produces Undef *)
Definition obs1' : history nat := 1 \-> 99.

Lemma obs_conflict_invalid : ~~ valid (obs1 \+ obs1').
Proof. by rewrite /obs1 /obs1'; compute. Qed.
\end{lstlisting}

These two lemmas capture the core safety property: valid composition is not
assumed or checked at runtime; it is established once, by proof, and then
inherited by any code that depends on the composed history.

\section{Discussion and Research Directions}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Alignment-Aware Planning}

The alignment problem for learned agents \citep{russell2019human,
gabriel2020alignment} is, in part, a problem of specification: how does an
agent know that the actions it is considering are within the scope of what it
is supposed to do? Current approaches rely heavily on reward shaping, RLHF
\citep{christiano2017rlhf, bai2022training}, and constitutional constraints
\citep{bai2022constitutional}, all of which are intrinsically extrinsic—they
surround the model with evaluative signals rather than embedding constraints
within its representational structure.

A proof-carrying world model offers a complementary approach. By requiring
that high-stakes action types carry typed witnesses of their preconditions, the
planning procedure is structurally prevented from producing plans that would
violate stated constraints—not because a guard intercepts the output, but
because the output type cannot be constructed without the required evidence. The
difference is analogous to the difference between a runtime bounds check and a
statically verified array access: the latter eliminates a class of error by
construction, before execution begins.

We envision a planning module whose action type is parameterized by a
capability scope $\kappa$: an action of type $\mathsf{Action}(\kappa)$ may
only exercise the capabilities enumerated in $\kappa$. The planner's type
signature then enforces that it cannot produce an action requiring capability
$c$ unless $c \in \kappa$. Scope escalation becomes a type error.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Trust and Fragment Provenance}

In multi-agent settings, observations arrive from diverse sources with varying
degrees of trust. A dependently typed world model can represent this by
annotating observation histories with \emph{provenance types}: a history of
type $\mathsf{Hist}(\tau)$ was collected under trust level $\tau$, and the
type system can enforce that a planner operating under trust level $\tau'$
may only act on histories whose provenance satisfies $\tau' \preceq \tau$ for
some trust ordering $\preceq$.

The PCM structure ensures that joining histories of incompatible provenance
produces an \texttt{Undef} result rather than a silently untrusted composite.
This makes cross-provenance contamination detectable at the type level—a
property that has practical significance wherever agents interact with
adversarially constructed inputs or aggregated data of heterogeneous origin.

\subsection{Connections to Verified Machine Learning}

Our proposal is broadly consistent with a growing literature on
\emph{verified machine learning} \citep{seshia2018formal, katz2019marabou,
liu2021algorithms} and \emph{certified programs}
\citep{leroy2009compcert, klein2009sel4}. These works pursue machine-checked
correctness for specific properties—robustness bounds, absence of undefined
behavior—but typically treat the neural component as a black box to be
analyzed from the outside. Our contribution is to argue that the
\emph{interface} of a world model—its observation operators, imagination
rollouts, and planning predicates—can itself be given a dependently typed
specification that interoperates naturally with proof-assistant toolchains.

This is complementary to, rather than in competition with, techniques for
verifying the \emph{internals} of neural networks. A world model whose
interface carries machine-checked guarantees can be composed with a certifying
planner even if the neural weights themselves remain opaque. The typed
interface becomes the contract between the learned and the verified parts of
the system.

\subsection{Toward a Full Formal Development}

The formalizations in \S\ref{sec:rocq} are illustrative sketches, not a
complete verified system. A full development would require:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Rocq formalization of the world model interface parameterized by an
    arbitrary PCM, with proofs of the stated closure properties.
  \item A typed rollout algebra: a Rocq structure capturing the sequential
    composition of proof-carrying transitions under horizon constraints.
  \item A formalization of capability scopes as PCMs over finite maps of
    authorization tokens, with lifting lemmas connecting scope composition to
    the planning type.
  \item An extraction pipeline enabling certified planning code to run inside
    a standard MBRL training loop.
\end{enumerate}

We leave this development as future work, noting that each component has
precedent: the FCSL-PCM library provides the algebraic foundations; verified
functional programs with extraction are routine in Rocq; and typed planning
interfaces have been explored in the context of verified task planners
\citep{blanchette2011nitpick}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{World models.}
Ha and Schmidhuber's World Models \citep{ha2018world} established the
``dream-then-act'' paradigm for visual planning. The DreamerV1--V3 series
\citep{hafner2019dream, hafner2021mastering, hafner2023mastering} demonstrated
that continuous control and Atari tasks can be mastered through pure
imagination. MuZero \citep{schrittwieser2020mastering} showed that a latent
model without ground-truth reconstruction suffices for search-based planning.
EfficientZero \citep{ye2021efficientzero} extended this to extreme sample
efficiency. None of these architectures address typed invariants on the model
interface.

\paragraph{Formal methods and learning.}
Seshia et al.\ \citep{seshia2018formal} survey formal specification and
verification techniques applicable to learning-enabled systems, emphasizing
runtime monitors and assume-guarantee contracts. Neural network verification
\citep{katz2019marabou, liu2021algorithms} focuses on robustness of fixed
networks. CompCert \citep{leroy2009compcert} and seL4 \citep{klein2009sel4}
demonstrate that large software systems can be given machine-checked
correctness proofs; our work proposes that learned interfaces can participate
in such developments.

\paragraph{Separation logic and resources.}
Reynolds \citep{reynolds2002sl} introduced separation logic; O'Hearn
\citep{ohearn2019sl} surveys two decades of development. Nanevski et al.\
\citep{nanevski2019fcsl} develop FCSL as a Hoare-logic framework for
concurrent separation reasoning, with a mature Rocq library. We repurpose this
infrastructure for compositional history reasoning.

\paragraph{Alignment and specification.}
Russell \citep{russell2019human} frames alignment as a problem of
misspecified objectives. Christiano et al.\ \citep{christiano2017rlhf} and
Bai et al.\ \citep{bai2022training, bai2022constitutional} address alignment
through human feedback and constitutional constraints. Gabriel
\citep{gabriel2020alignment} provides a conceptual taxonomy. Our proposal is
orthogonal: rather than specifying objectives more accurately, we propose to
specify the operational interface of the model more precisely.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We have argued that the latent states, observation operators, and planning
procedures of learned world models are appropriate targets for dependent type
annotation, and that partial commutative monoids provide a natural algebraic
language for the compositional reasoning this requires. The resulting notion
of a \emph{proof-carrying world model} shifts certain safety and scope
guarantees from runtime monitors and external policy tables to the type
structure of the model itself—making violations detectable before execution
rather than after.

The proposal is not a complete solution to the deep challenges of learned-agent
reliability. A world model whose interface is well-typed may still learn an
inaccurate model of environment dynamics; proof-carrying state does not
substitute for empirical validity. What it does is ensure that the commitments
the system makes about scope, provenance, and composition are expressed in a
form that can be checked mechanically, composed reliably, and depended upon by
downstream verified components.

In that modest but precise sense, dependent type theory offers machine
learning something it has rarely been asked to accept: not a new performance
benchmark, but an obligation to say clearly what it knows—and to carry the
proof.
